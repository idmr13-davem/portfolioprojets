Sign-up inMercado Libre

Role
UX senior analyst, UI designer
Period
Q3 2023 → Q4 2025
Status
Live

Objectives

  • Increase flow conversion.
  • Reduce completion time.

Outcomes

  • Reduce average completion time from 3 minutes and 25 seconds to 52 seconds.

Context

When I took on this project, Mercado Libre and Mercado Pago shared a single sign-up flow, despite having fundamentally different conversion goals.

Mercado Pago, a fintech product, needed flows that met each country’s regulatory requirements: strict identity verification, mandatory account-type selection and KYC compliance. Mercado Libre needed account creation to be fast enough to preserve purchase intent.

The strategic decision to split the teams made everything that followed possible. This case documents the Mercado Libre side: four cumulative changes over two years that transformed a broken flow into the standard the team still uses.

Challenges

  • Create a new sign-up experience aligned with Mercado Libre’s goals.
  • Remove friction during shopping.
  • Unify sign-up paths into a modular flow.

Agnostic sign-up

Iteration

Mercado Libre asked buyers to choose between “personal” and “business” accounts—a distinction inherited from the fintech product’s regulatory requirements. The decision was to remove account-type selection entirely from Mercado Libre’s sign-up flow.

Impact

  • Conversion increased by 2%.
  • Monthly registrations increased by 160,000 across a monthly audience of 8 million users.

Sign-up form

Iteration

The existing flow was linear, with five steps and ten screens. It was built around data validation rather than fast completion.

I separated data entry from validation: all information would be collected on one screen, with phone verification on the following screen. This reduced the flow from ten screens to three.

Removing identity validation also required a new approach to abuse prevention, using reCAPTCHA instead.

Impact

  • Sign-up completion time fell from 3:25 to 1:54 minutes.
  • Account activation increased from 18% to 27%.
  • Abuse decreased from 5.04% to 4.92%.

Sign-up with Google

Iteration

Even with the new form, people still had to enter their name, email, password and phone number manually. This was slow and error-prone, especially on mobile.

I implemented Google Identity Services: the Google button, One Tap and Credential Manager, following Google’s guidelines. One-click data entry increased conversion and reduced sign-up time further.

Impact

  • Sign-up completion time fell from 1:54 minutes to 50 seconds.
  • Conversion increased by 0.6% compared with the sign-up form.

What remains to be done?

Remove phone verification. A product requirement prevented us from removing this step. The design notes estimate a ten-second average without it; this remains an unvalidated opportunity, not an achieved result.

Unified sign-up and login

Iteration

After social sign-up launched, abandonment decreased but the friction changed. People entering sign-up ended up in login, and vice versa. Switching between flows increased from 16% to 41%.

Together with user research, this showed that people were not thinking about signing in or creating an account—they simply wanted to get in. I unified both flows into a single entry screen that routed people based on the information they entered.

Impact

  • Switching between flows decreased from 41% to 18%.
  • Abandonment decreased and conversion increased by 23%.

What did I focus on?

Usability testing revealed that typing errors were a risk in the unified screen. We tested several solutions with people and defined a pill component that kept their entered information visible throughout the flow.

Final outcomes

After two years working on this project, the main outcomes were:

  • Reduce completion time from over three minutes to 50 seconds.
  • Make signing in and creating an account easier during checkout.
  • Make account access easier, with an experience consistent with other marketplaces in Latin America, such as Temu and Shopee.
