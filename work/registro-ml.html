Mercado Libre

UX senior analyst, UI designer
Q3 2023 → Q4 2025
Productive Visit project

UX objectives

Increase flow conversion

Reduce completion time

Our most important achievement

Reduced average completion time from 3 minutes and 25 seconds to 52 seconds

Context

When I took ownership of this project, registration was a single shared flow for both Mercado Libre and Mercado Pago — two products with fundamentally different conversion goals.

Mercado Pago (fintech) needed flows that met regulatory requirements for each country: strict identity verification, mandatory account type selection, KYC compliance. Mercado Libre (marketplace) needed accounts created fast enough that friction didn't kill purchase intent.

The strategic decision to split the teams was the prerequisite for everything that followed. What I'm documenting here is the Mercado Libre side — four compounding changes over two years that turned a broken flow into the standard the team still ships against.

Faced challenges

  • Create a complete new signup flow aligned with our new goal in Mercado Libre.
  • Stop being a friction in middle of purchases.
  • Unify the different kind of signup flows in one which operate modularly.

Our compounding changes

Mercado Libre was asking buyers to choose between "personal" and "business" accounts — a bifurcation that existed only because fintech required it for regulatory reasons. The decision was to remove the account type selection from the Mercado Libre flow entirely. A buyer doesn't need to categorize themselves before they can shop. This was only possible because we had split the teams — the same change would have broken Mercado Pago's compliance requirements.

Impact

+2% conversion rate, 160,000 additional monthly registrations on 8M users/month.

The existing flow was linear by design: enter a piece of data, validate it via OTP or identity challenge, wait for the system, move to the next screen. Five steps, ten screens. The model was built around verification, not conversion. We separated declaration from validation: all required fields on a single screen, with only phone number validated via OTP afterward. This collapsed 5 steps and 10 screens into 2 steps and 3 screens. Removing step-by-step validation meant rethinking abuse control. We implemented ReCaptcha at the form level and at the OTP screen — lower friction without losing the detection layer.

Impact

3:25 → 1:54 completion time, 18% → 27% activation rate, abuse dropped from 5.04% to 4.92%.

Even with the form, users still had to manually type name, email, password, and phone. Manual data entry is inherently error-prone and friction-generating especially on mobile. The solution was to implement Google Identity Services: the Google button, One Tap, and Credential Manager. With a verified Google account we pre-fill name and email, eliminate the password field, and reduce the flow to a single OTP.

Impact

+0.6pp conversion vs form-only, 1:54 minutes → 50 seconds completion time.

What we left on the table

We couldn't eliminate phone OTP because it was the only validated recovery data. With Google, the validated data point becomes email. If shipped, registration drops to ~10 seconds via One Tap.

As we reduced friction, drop rates fell, but transformed. The switch rate between registration and login jumped from 16% to 41.4% after social was introduced. Social made registration so fast that users tried it impulsively before checking which flow they were in.

The reframe

Users don't have a task of "registering" or "logging in." They have a task of accessing Mercado Libre. ML sessions last 6+ months on the same device. Many users hadn't typed credentials in so long they couldn't confidently identify which flow they needed.

The solution was to implement one screen that accepts an identifier (email or phone), the screen logic determines whether an account exists, and routes accordingly to sign-in or sign-up. The user never has to decide which path they're on.

The risk we designed around

Typos could route a user to the wrong flow and accidentally create a duplicate account. Solution: a persistent pill displaying the entered identifier in subsequent screens — turning a passive input into an active confirmation point.

Final result

At the moment I left this project the new unification flow hadn't been implemented, I didn't get information about the performance of the new flow productively, but part of the result was:

  • Reduce the time completion significantly from 3:25 minutes to 50 seconds.
  • Increase flow conversion by 12.5% and 180k new users per month when unification was implemented in specific sites like Brazil.
