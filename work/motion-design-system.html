Motion Level -
Motion design system

Role: Senior UI designer, researcher
Q4 2024 → Q2 2025
Implemented

Objectives

  • Standardize the use of motion at Mercado Libre.
  • Establish shared criteria for motion and expressivity across design teams.

Outcomes

  • Contributed to the initial foundations for motion.
  • Created Motion Level, a framework for shared motion decisions.

Context

Motion decisions depended on individual judgment. I joined as an interaction analyst to research concepts, criteria and practices that could inform a future design system.

My contribution focused on research and the initial framework before handing the work to a specialist motion team.

Challenges

  • Component-level patterns existed, but custom motion lacked shared criteria.
  • The team needed a repeatable process for designing animations.
  • Limited specialist capacity made collaboration difficult.
  • Designers needed guidance to take ownership of simpler motion work.
  • Delivery demands left limited space to define shared foundations.

Roadmap

The initial work moved from research to foundations, documentation and expansion, with contributions from UX and motion designers.

Building shared criteria with Motion Level

After the research, we began defining motion principles, standardizing easing, timing and screen transitions, and establishing shared decision criteria.

I proposed Motion Level to guide animation intensity by comparing expressivity with how frequently an animation appears.

What do we mean by expressivity?

How much emotion or excitement should an animation convey?

What does frequency mean?

How often someone encounters an animation in their journey.

High-frequency animations support common tasks, such as updating details or making a purchase.

Low-frequency animations are reserved for milestones, such as reaching a sales goal or acquiring a new product.

Left end:

Reserve these animations for meaningful milestones in a person’s journey, for example:

  • Reaching adulthood.

  • Reaching a sales milestone.

Right end:

Use these animations for everyday moments, such as:

  • Making a purchase.
  • Updating a detail.
  • Uploading a photo.

Final outcomes

  • The framework translates research into shared criteria for functional, balanced and expressive motion.
  • After my initial contribution, a specialist motion team continued the technical development of the design system.
