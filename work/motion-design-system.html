Objectives

  • Standardize the use of motion at Mercado Libre.
  • Establish shared criteria for motion and expressivity across design teams.

Outcomes

  • Defined the initial motion foundations at Mercado Libre.
  • Created Motion Level, a framework that brings shared criteria to motion decisions across design teams.

Context

For years, motion experiences at Mercado Libre relied on a small number of specialists working on demand. Most other experiences used basic screen transitions or component-specific easing and timing for elements such as bottom sheets and snackbars.

I joined as an interaction analyst to research concepts, criteria and practices that could inform a future system. I contributed for nine months before handing the project to the first dedicated motion design team, helping establish the initial foundations of Mercado Libre’s motion design system.

Challenges

  • Only some components had standardized animations—for example, bottom sheets shared easing and duration.
  • There was no shared guidance on when to use custom motion; each team made its own decisions.
  • Projects involving motion lacked a clear methodology.
  • At the outset, there was roughly one motion designer for every 80 designers. This created a bottleneck. Without clear guidance, giving designers ownership of simpler motion projects led to inconsistent results.
  • Motion designers focused on delivery, leaving limited time to build and define the system.

Roadmap

The initial stage followed these steps. Three analysts shared the work: two UX designers and a motion designer who joined during phase two.

  2. Define foundations
  3. Create documentation

Building shared criteria with Motion Level

After completing the initial research, we began defining motion principles, standardizing easing, timing and screen transitions, and establishing shared decision criteria. I took on the shared criteria and proposed Motion Level: a framework that helps determine animation intensity by comparing expressivity with frequency of appearance.

What do we mean by expressivity?

How much emotion or excitement should an animation convey?

What does frequency mean?

  • How often someone encounters an animation in their journey.
  • High-frequency animations support common tasks, such as updating details or making a purchase.
  • Low-frequency animations are reserved for milestones, such as reaching a sales goal or acquiring a new product.

With these axes in place, we defined the following points:

+ Expressivity− Expressivity− Frequency+ Frequency

Left end:

  • Reserve these animations for key milestones in a person’s journey, for example:
  • Turning 18 after using an account as a minor.
  • Acquiring a first financial product.
  • Reaching the first 100k in sales.

Right end:

  • Use these animations for everyday moments in a person’s journey:
  • Making a purchase.
  • Updating a detail.
  • Uploading a photo.

Final outcomes

  • The framework became part of the design principles through functional, balanced and expressive design. It aligned criteria across design teams so that animation expressivity matched the task, frequency of use and impact on a person’s journey.
  • After my participation in the initial stage, a specialist motion team continued evolving the project and developing its technical foundations.
